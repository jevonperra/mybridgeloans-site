HELOC vs cash-out refinance vs hard money 2nd

There are three ways to pull equity out of a property you already own. Two are bank products and one is private money. The right one depends on how fast you need the money, whether your income and credit document, whether you want to keep your current first mortgage, and what the money is for. MyBridge Loans brokers HELOCs and cash-out refinances in California and funds hard money 2nd trust deeds directly, so the answer is whichever fits, not whichever we sell.

Pick a HELOC when

Your income documents, your FICO is above the lender's floor, and you want money available to draw over time rather than one lump sum. It is the cheapest way to tap equity for a borrower a bank is comfortable with. MyBridge Loans brokers HELOCs in California to the lender whose guidelines fit the file.

Pick a cash-out refinance when

Your current first mortgage is at a rate higher than today's, so replacing it costs you nothing extra, or you want one payment instead of two. If your first is at a low rate from 2020 or 2021, a cash-out refinance means paying today's rate on the entire balance just to reach the equity. Run that number before you do it.

Pick a hard money 2nd when

  • The bank declined the HELOC: self-employed, income that does not document, FICO below the floor, a recent credit event, or a property type the bank will not lend on.
  • You need the money in two weeks, not six.
  • You want to keep a low-rate first mortgage exactly as it is.
  • The use is a business purpose: buying an investment property, capital for a business, paying business taxes or debt, a partner buyout.

It costs more per month than a HELOC. The trade is certainty and speed. Most borrowers who take a hard money 2nd plan to pay it off from a sale, a refinance once the credit or income issue clears, or a business event. See hard money 2nd trust deeds for the full terms.

The math on keeping the first

Property worth $1,200,000 with a $500,000 first at 3%. You need $200,000. A cash-out refinance to $700,000 at a current rate reprices all $700,000. A HELOC or 2nd prices only the $200,000. On a $500,000 first, the difference between 3% and a current rate is tens of thousands of dollars a year. That gap often pays for a hard money 2nd several times over, even at its higher rate.
