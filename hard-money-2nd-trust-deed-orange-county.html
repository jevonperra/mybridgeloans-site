Hard money 2nd trust deed in Orange County, CA

MyBridge Loans funds private money second trust deeds in Orange County and Los Angeles up to 65% combined loan-to-value, behind an existing first mortgage that stays in place. Business purpose only. Same-day answer on any scenario. Typical funding in two weeks.

How the numbers work

Take the as-is value, multiply by 65%, subtract the first mortgage balance. That is the maximum second. Example: $1,200,000 value × 65% = $780,000. First mortgage of $500,000. Maximum 2nd trust deed: $280,000. Need more? Cross a second property and the combined loan can go higher.

What we need to say yes

  • The first mortgage is current, with no lates in the last 12 months and no forbearance.
  • The use of funds is a business purpose: a business, an investment property, business debt, or taxes tied to a business or investment.
  • A clear exit: sale, refinance, or a business event that pays the loan off.
  • Clean title. Tax liens and judgments can usually be paid through escrow.

What does not matter much

FICO, income documentation, and self-employment. There is no minimum score, no income verification, and no seasoning. The loan is priced on the position and the equity. A higher score prices better, but a lower score with strong equity and a clean first-mortgage history still funds. Foreign nationals and ITIN borrowers qualify with a tax ID or entity EIN and a U.S. bank account, at about 5% less leverage.

Rates and cost

Second trust deeds run 11.99% to 12.99% and up, interest-only, usually 1.5 to 2 lender points plus broker, on a 3-year note with a 3 to 6 month prepayment penalty. Loans from $100,000 to $4,000,000 and up, $4,000 minimum origination. Exceptions available. There is one broker fee in the deal because we fund through our own investors.

Fees: underwriting $1,995. No processing fee. No doc prep fee in California. Appraisal by a third-party appraiser, commonly around $700. Up to 30 days of prepaid interest at closing. Credit report, title, escrow, and flood at whatever those third parties charge.

Common uses

  • Down payment on an investment property or a business acquisition.
  • Paying an IRS or FTB lien so a sale or refinance can close.
  • Buying out a partner, sibling, or ex-spouse's interest in a rental.
  • Working capital against a rental or commercial building with a low-rate first you don't want to lose.
